Glioma is a type of tumor that starts in the glial cells of the brain or spine.
Glial cells support and protect neurons in the brain.
Gliomas are classified by grade: low-grade (slow growing) and high-grade (fast growing, aggressive).
Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive form of glioma and is classified as grade 4.
Symptoms include headaches, nausea, seizures, memory problems, and personality changes.
Treatment typically involves surgery, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy.
Temozolomide is a common chemotherapy drug used for glioblastoma.
The prognosis depends on the grade, location, and the patient's age and health.
Low-grade gliomas may be managed for years, while high-grade gliomas have a more serious outlook.
Genetic markers like IDH mutation and MGMT methylation influence treatment response and prognosis.
