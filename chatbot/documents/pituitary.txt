Pituitary tumors are abnormal growths that develop in the pituitary gland.
The pituitary gland is a small gland at the base of the brain that controls many hormones in the body.
Most pituitary tumors are benign (non-cancerous) and are called pituitary adenomas.
They can be functioning (hormone-secreting) or non-functioning (not producing excess hormones).
Functioning tumors can cause hormonal imbalances leading to conditions like Cushing's disease or acromegaly.
Symptoms include headaches, vision problems (especially loss of peripheral vision), fatigue, and hormonal issues.
Treatment options include medication, surgery (transsphenoidal surgery), and radiation therapy.
Transsphenoidal surgery is performed through the nose and is minimally invasive.
Most pituitary tumors respond well to treatment and the prognosis is generally favorable.
Regular hormone level monitoring and MRI scans are needed after treatment.
